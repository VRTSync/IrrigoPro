    <h2>What changed in this revision</h2>
    <p class="lede">
      Findings are out. Valuing them meant reaching into mid-flight objects &mdash; half of them already routed to an estimate or work order,
      some sitting on wet checks a tech has not submitted, and on flat-mode checks their labour hours are zero by design, so the money would have been wrong.
    </p>
    <p class="lede">
      A work order is the dispatchable unit. It has a customer, a status, a scheduled date and a real dollar total written at creation.
      A wet check is the other thing you send a crew to do. Those two, plus the budget, are the whole board &mdash; and it deletes the two hardest
      questions from the last revision: no cut algorithm, no severity tiebreaker.
    </p>

      <div class="co">
        <b>Work order values are written at creation, not at completion.</b>
        Both estimate&rarr;work-order conversion paths compute <code>estimatedTotal</code> from the estimate&rsquo;s items and its snapshotted <code>appliedLaborRate</code>, handling flat and per-part labour correctly. So an open work order carries a real number before anyone touches a valve &mdash; which is what makes this board possible and findings not.
      </div>
      <div class="co">
        <b>Direct work orders carry no estimate.</b>
        <code>workType</code> can be <code>direct_billing</code> or <code>maintenance</code> &mdash; created without an estimate, so <code>estimatedTotal</code> is null. Ashford Park shows the handling: <em>&ldquo;1 &middot; no estimate&rdquo;</em>, never <em>$0</em>. A blank that reads as zero would tell the manager a job is free.

  <section class="sec">
    <h2>Over budget does not mean stay home</h2>
    <p class="lede">
      <b>Decided 2 September:</b> a customer over their monthly budget still gets their wet check &mdash; but it runs in <b>inspection</b> mode.
      The tech documents and does not repair, and the visit comes back as an estimate for the HOA to approve rather than a bill they never agreed to.
      The monthly standard is kept, nothing is billed against a spent budget, and the work that needs doing is captured and priced instead of forgotten.
    </p>

    <div class="callouts" style="margin-top:0">
      <div class="co">
        <b>This is the mode split the wet-check redesign already built.</b>
        Service produces repairs and a snapshot to bill; inspection produces an estimate. Both are live in the code today &mdash; the create gate takes
        <code>mode: "service" | "inspection"</code>, the finding sheet branches on <code>wetCheckMode</code>, the review surface has an inspection path,
        and <code>inspection-estimate-items.ts</code> builds the estimate. Nothing new has to be invented to honour this rule.
      </div>
      <div class="co">
        <b>Enforce it at capture, not on the board.</b>
        Under Rule 4 a guard belongs where the data is created. <code>wet-check-create-gate.ts</code> already holds one pure gate of this shape
        (branch required for multi-location customers); this becomes a second: resolve the customer&rsquo;s headroom server-side and force
        <code>mode = "inspection"</code> when it is at or below zero. The Action Board then only <em>reports</em> the rule &mdash; it never has to enforce it,
        and a tech who starts a check from the field gets the same answer.
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="sec">
    <h2>Approved estimates sit outside the budget</h2>
    <p class="lede">
      <b>Decided 2 September.</b> The monthly allocation governs discretionary work &mdash; what the crew decides to do on a visit.
      Work the HOA has seen and approved is not discretionary, so it does not consume the allocation. That closes the loop the inspection rule opened:
      an over-budget property gets an inspection, the inspection becomes an estimate, the HOA approves it, and the resulting work order can be done
      without waiting for October.
    </p>
    <p class="lede">
      On the board this is why Sunrise Estates sits in <em>Clear to send</em> at 169% of budget. Its two work orders came from approved estimates, so
      they are marked <em>pre-approved &mdash; outside budget</em> and the do-not-exceed reads <b>$0 + pre-approved</b>: no discretionary money, but
      approved work to get on with.
    </p>

    <div class="callouts" style="margin-top:0">
      <div class="co">
        <b>The board half is easy.</b>
        A work order is exempt when <code>workOrders.estimateId</code> is set &mdash; both conversion paths only create one from an already-approved estimate.
        Everything else, <code>direct_billing</code> and <code>maintenance</code> work orders, is discretionary and counts. That is a single nullable FK, already there.
      </div>
      <div class="co">
        <b>Account for it; do not exempt it.</b>
        The customer really has been billed $3,040 against an $1,800 allocation &mdash; that is true and worth knowing. What is different is whether it is a problem,
        and it is not, because they approved it. So <code>computeCustomerSpend</code> gains a fourth number rather than changing the three it already returns.
      </div>
    </div>

    <p class="lede" style="margin-top:22px">
      <b>The agreed shape.</b> <code>computeCustomerSpend</code> returns <code>{ invoiced, pendingNotBilled, total }</code> today. Add <code>preApproved</code> &mdash;
      the estimate-derived portion. Every existing caller keeps reading <code>total</code> and nothing changes. Surfaces that care about discretionary headroom
      subtract it: the Action Board computes do-not-exceed as <code>allocation &minus; (total &minus; preApproved)</code>, and Budget Status can later show
      <em>&ldquo;Over &mdash; approved&rdquo;</em> instead of <em>Stop</em> when the whole overage is pre-approved. Additive, not a redefinition: no retroactive
      movement in past months, no three-surface blast radius, and each screen opts in when it is ready.
    </p>
    <p class="lede">
      The data supports it. <code>invoice_items</code> carries <code>sourceType</code> with typed foreign keys, so an invoice&rsquo;s items trace back through
      <code>workOrders.estimateId</code> to the estimate; and invoices carry no discount, tax or adjustment column, so a total decomposes into its items cleanly.
    </p>
    <p class="lede">
      <b>Not now, though (decided 2 September).</b> Nothing in the system has a budget yet, so nobody can hit this case. It goes third &mdash; after the restores
      and after the goals are entered. The reason it is written down here is so whoever builds the board knows the do-not-exceed number is provisional and
      where the seam is, rather than hard-coding the assumption that spend is spend.
    </p>
  </section>
