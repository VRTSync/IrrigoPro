# AMENDMENT to #2040 — date the month by `startedAt`, not `submittedAt`

One change. Everything else in #2040 stands, including the catches it made that the source spec missed.

## The decision to reverse

#2040 says the due decision "reads a submitted-or-later check and dates it by `submittedAt`, not by `startedAt`", and warns that the existing newest-wet-check queries order by `startedAt` so "reusing one unexamined gives the wrong answer."

**The status half is right. The date half is backwards, and the warning is inverted.**

Correct rule: **status must be `submitted` or later; the month is decided by `startedAt`.**

## Why — the reason that decides it

`wetChecks.startedAt` is `defaultNow().notNull()` (`schema.ts:1534`). It is always present.

`wetChecks.submittedAt` is nullable (`schema.ts:1535`), **and there is a production path that sets the status to `submitted` without ever setting it**:

```
// migrations/0005-reconcile-approved-wet-checks.ts:113-116
if (buckets.submitted.length > 0) {
  await tx.update(wetChecks)
    .set({ status: "submitted", updatedAt: new Date() })
    .where(inArray(wetChecks.id, buckets.submitted));
}
```

Every wet check that reconcile migration touched carries `status = 'submitted'` and `submittedAt = null`. Dating the month by `submittedAt` drops all of them out of every month — those customers read as never having had a check, permanently, and no amount of doing the work fixes it. A null date in a month comparison either excludes the row silently or throws, and neither failure is visible on the board.

`storage.ts:11323-11329` also flips a wet check back to `submitted` when a converted estimate is reverted, without touching `submittedAt`. Less dangerous, since such a row was submitted once before, but it confirms the column is not maintained as an invariant.

## Why — the reason that makes it correct anyway

The standard is one wet check per customer per month, and the thing being counted is **the visit**. A tech on the property on 30 September did September's visit; paperwork landing on 1 October does not move it. Dating by `submittedAt` puts that visit in October, so September reads as missed forever and October reads as satisfied by a visit that never happened there — two errors from one off-by-a-day.

## The consequence for the implementation, restated

- The status filter is the new part: only `submitted`, `approved`, `partially_converted` or `converted` count. A row at `in_progress` never satisfies the month, however recently it was started. That is the abandoned-check case, and it is the whole reason the status gate exists.
- **The date column is not the new part.** The existing newest-wet-check queries already order by `startedAt` and that is correct — reuse it. What they lack is the status filter, not a different date.
- The completed-state text on the row ("Done 1 Sep", "last 24 Jul") shows the **`startedAt`** date, so the date the manager reads is the day the crew was there, and it is the same fact the lane applied.
- A check started 30 September and submitted 15 October shows September as due until it is submitted, then satisfies September. That flip is correct: the month was genuinely unsatisfied until the work was confirmed done.

## Add to the tests

- A wet check with `status = 'submitted'` and `submittedAt = null` — the migration-0005 shape — still satisfies its month. This is the regression that proves the column choice.
- A check with `startedAt` on the last day of the month and `submittedAt` in the next month satisfies the **earlier** month, and does not satisfy the later one.
- A check at `in_progress` started inside the month leaves the month due (already in #2040 — keep it).
