# #2019 — Financial Pulse: Attribution and Double-Counts

## What & Why

Three drill-downs report more money than the company made, because each credits the same dollar to more than one row. Against a Billed MTD of **$47,782**:

| Surface | Reported | Ratio | Captured |
|---|---|---|---|
| By Technician, Revenue column (4 techs) | $110,526 | 2.31x | 2026-09-11 |
| Pulse tab, technician YTD column (6 techs) | $636,462 against ~$309,469 invoiced YTD | 2.06x | 2026-09-11 |
| Revenue mix, "Parts vs Labor" donut total | $92,803 | 1.94x | 2026-09-15 |
| By Service Type, "% of total" column | 200% | 2.00x | 2026-09-11 |

The In-Flight column on the same technician table sums to $23,623 against a tile of $23,624, so the attribution machinery is not broken everywhere. It is specifically the crediting of **invoice** revenue that duplicates: each record type is joined to its invoice, and then the whole invoice is credited to every row that touched it.

## Done looks like

- The By Technician Revenue column sums to Billed MTD for the same company and period, within rounding. The Pulse tab's technician YTD column sums to Invoiced YTD.
- No invoice contributes more than its `totalAmount` to any drill-down, on any tab.
- The "Parts vs Labor" donut counts each wet check's parts and labor exactly once.
- Each service-type group's percentages sum to 100%, and the two groups are visibly separate rather than four rows under one header.
- An invoice billed at the emergency rate is classified from the ticket's own rate mode, not from matching a rate to two decimal places.
- The By Technician table has no Margin % column, in the UI or the CSV, and `marginPct` is gone from the row type.
- Labor cost stays exactly as it is.

## Decisions already made — implement these, do not relitigate

- **Invoice revenue is split proportionally by hours** across the technicians credited to that invoice. When the contributing rows carry no usable hours, split equally. A single-technician invoice is unchanged — that is the common case.
- **The technician table must reconcile to the billed total.** That is a test, not a comment.
- **Margin % is deleted, not hidden.** It is empty in production today — no user has an `hourlyWage`, so every row renders "—" — and it subtracts labor but not parts, on the same page as a "Profit Margin" tile that subtracts both. Two unqualified margins that disagree is the defect this series exists to close. Shipping a half-corrected version would be the first time that column ever displayed a number, and it would be wrong. Hiding it behind a flag pending another ticket is a deferred problem and a crutch (Rule 8) — remove it.
- **Labor cost stays.** It is correct but unpopulated: a real cost that appears the moment wages are entered. Margin % is wrong by construction whether or not wages exist. Delete the structurally incomplete one; keep the one waiting on data.
- **Per-technician margin returns only once a real per-line parts cost exists** — the parts-cost work adds it back as a deliberate feature, computed the same way the tile is. It is not restored by this ticket.
- **Emergency classification comes from `rateMode`**, with the existing rate comparison kept only as a fallback for rows predating that column.

## Out of scope

- The Profit Margin tile's own cost bases. That is the parts-cost ticket; this one only removes the technician table's competing margin.
- The parts-vs-labor stacked chart being empty before 2026-04, and the revenue trend's year-over-year asymmetry.
- Whether `partsSubtotal + laborSubtotal` should equal `totalAmount`. It does not for older invoices with null subtotals — a data question, not a mix question.
- Attributing invoice revenue to technicians anywhere outside these two tables.

## Steps

1. **Split invoice revenue across the technicians who earned it.** Build the per-invoice technician shares from the work orders, billing sheets and wet-check billings already loaded for the window — each carries hours and a technician. Allocate the invoice's `totalAmount` in proportion to hours; where an invoice's contributing rows have no usable hours, split it equally; where it has one technician, that technician takes all of it. Apply the identical allocation to `partsRevenue`. No new query is needed — the three loaders already return hours and technician ids.

2. **Apply the same allocation on the Pulse tab.** `computePulseTechnicians` credits whole invoices through its `creditInvoice` helper; replace that with the shared allocation so the Pulse tab's YTD column reconciles to Invoiced YTD. Leave the in-flight accumulation alone — it already attributes correctly, which is why that column sums to its tile today.

3. **Rename what avg ticket now means.** With fractional attribution, avg ticket is attributed revenue over invoices worked, not the value of a whole ticket. Retitle the column and its CSV header to "Avg per invoice worked" so it is not read as a ticket value.

4. **Remove the Margin % column.** Delete `marginPct` from the technician row type, from the table header and body, and from the CSV header and row — all three together, or dead data stays in the API for a later surface to pick up. Leave Labor cost and its `hasWageSet` flag untouched.

5. **Stop re-adding invoiced wet-check subtotals to the revenue mix.** Invoice generation already builds `invoices.partsSubtotal` and `laborSubtotal` as work orders plus billing sheets plus wet-check billings, so the mix's invoiced-WCB loop counts every invoiced wet check's parts and labor a second time. Delete that loop, and delete the collection in the route that feeds it — which also removes an O(n²) scan over a year of invoice ids. The **uninvoiced** wet-check leg is correct and stays: that work is in no invoice yet. Remove the parameter from the helper's signature rather than passing an empty array, so nothing can quietly start feeding it again.

6. **Render service type as two splits.** Each row gains a group — urgency for emergency/standard, agreement for contract/adhoc — and `pctOfTotal` is computed **within the row's own group** so each sums to 100%. The table renders two labelled sections rather than four rows under one header; the CSV gains the same group column. The `# invoices` column stops double-counting for the same reason.

7. **Classify emergency from the ticket, not the rate.** Work orders and billing sheets both carry `rateMode` ('normal' | 'emergency'), which is the authoritative record of how the work was billed. Classify from it; fall back to the existing rate comparison only for rows that predate the column; and treat a customer whose emergency rate equals their standard rate as never emergency, since the comparison cannot distinguish them.

8. **Tests.** Cover: one $1,000 invoice with two technicians at 6h and 2h giving $750 and $250 (the regression for the 2.31x — both got $1,000 before); the same invoice with no hours recorded giving $500 each; a single-technician invoice unchanged; the sum invariant, that the technician Revenue column equals billed revenue for the window to the cent and the Pulse YTD column equals Invoiced YTD; an invoiced wet-check billing contributing its parts and labor exactly once to the mix, and an uninvoiced one exactly once; each service-type group summing to 100% with each invoice appearing once per group; an invoice whose source ticket has `rateMode: "emergency"` classified emergency even when its labor rate matches the standard rate, and a customer with equal rates classified standard; and tenancy, so attribution never crosses companies.

## Test placement

Put this ticket's new tests in a file of its own; do not append to `financial-pulse.test.ts` or `financial-pulse-math.test.ts`, which several tickets each touch. **Add the new file to the `seasonal-budget-financial-pulse` validation command in `.replit`** — that command names its test files explicitly, so an unlisted file never runs in validation even though the package script globs `src/**/*.test.ts`. Note `financial-pulse-math.test.ts` is itself absent from that command, so pure-math assertions belong in this ticket's own registered file, not there. Do not add a workflow; this repo is past the workflow cap.

Prefer pure-math assertions — they need no database. Where a route test is required, assert invariants rather than absolutes: a column summing to its total, two endpoints returning the same number. Exact-count assertions like `equal(count, 3)` fail on rows left by other suites in the shared dev database and read as a regression in this ticket when they are not one.
